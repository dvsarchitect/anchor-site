**Advent Week 5: Living the Incarnation**  
**Scripture:** 1 John 4:7-11

---

### THE POST-CHRISTMAS CRASH

The wrapping paper is in the trash. The leftovers are in Tupperware. The guests are gone, and if you’re like me, there’s a quietness in the house today that feels less like "peace" and more like a biological crash.

We spend a month building up to the "Big Day." We sing the carols, we do the Advent candles, we stir up the sentiment. But now it’s December 26th. The "magic" is fading. The adrenaline is gone.

And this is exactly where most of us lose the plot.

We treat Christmas like a destination—a finish line. But in the Kingdom of God, the Manger wasn't a finish line. It was a beachhead. It was the moment God secured a footing in enemy territory so He could begin the work of total reclamation.

If we leave Jesus in the manger, we’ve missed the point. The Incarnation isn't just a mystery to be marveled at; it’s a life to be lived through.

The question isn't "Did you have a good Christmas?" The question is: "Now that God has taken on flesh, how are you going to live in yours?"

Today, we’re looking at 1 John 4. This is where the theology of the Incarnation gets tactical. This is where "God is love" stops being a bumper sticker and starts being a battle plan.

---

### THE CONTEXT: THE TACTICS OF LOVE

John is writing to a community that is under fire—not just from the outside, but from the inside. False teachers are trying to water down the Gospel, making Jesus into a ghost or a distant idea.

John won't have it. He keeps dragging them back to the *physicality* of Christ. Because if Christ didn't really come in the flesh, then God’s love is just an idea. And an idea can't save you when you're bleeding.

John is the "Apostle of Love," but don't mistake that for sentimentality. In the Greek, his writing is sharp, binary, and intellectually rigorous. He’s not talking about a feeling; he’s talking about an ontological shift in the human person.

---

### THE SCRIPTURE: 1 John 4:7-11

> "Dear friends, let us love one another, for love comes from God. Everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God. Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love. This is how God showed his love among us: He sent his one and only Son into the world that we might live through him. This is love: not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as an atoning sacrifice for our sins. Dear friends, since God so loved us, we also ought to love one another."
